Method & assumptions

Phase 1 model: steady, one-dimensional longitudinal ventilation of a single tunnel bore between end A (chainage 0) and end B. Positive velocity is from A to B. The solver finds the air velocity v at which the pressure sources balance the losses.

Pressure terms

  • Tunnel: ΔP = (λ L / Dh + ζin + ζout + Σζ) · ρ/2 · v|v|, Dh = 4A / P.
  • Stalled train: flow through the annulus Aa = A − At at va = v A / Aa, friction on Da = 4Aa / (P + Pt), contraction 0.5 r and Borda–Carnot expansion r² with r = 1 − Aa/A; the plain-tunnel friction over the train length is removed so it is not counted twice.
  • Jet fans: ΔP = n · η · T / A · (1 − v / vj) with rated thrust T.
  • End fan plant: n identical fans in parallel on a quadratic curve through the shut-off pressure and the rated point, minus the shaft & damper loss ∝ Q². A plant with no fans running adds nothing (dampers assumed to leave the portal open).
  • Portal pressure: wind or barometric difference between the ends, positive pushing A → B.
  • Fire buoyancy: (ρ0 − ρf) g s Ld over the hot length Ld from the fire to the downstream end, with the bulk smoke temperature Tf = T0 + χ Q / (ρ0 cp A v).

Critical velocity

  • Kennedy (NFPA 502 Annex D, NFPA 130): Vc = Kg · 0.61 · (g H Q / (ρ cp A Tf))^(1/3), Tf = Q / (ρ cp A Vc) + T0, iterated; Kg = 1 + 0.0374 · grade^0.8 when the smoke flows downhill (grade in %).
  • Li, Lei & Ingason (2010): Q* = Q / (ρ cp T0 g^½ H^2.5); V* = 0.81 Q*^(1/3) for Q* ≤ 0.15, otherwise 0.43; Vc = V* (gH)^½.
  • A fire scenario passes when the velocity is in the required smoke direction and at least Vc × (1 + margin).

Not in Phase 1

Moving trains and piston effect, transient fire growth and smoke spread, multiple bores, cross-passages, shafts along the tunnel, station and platform smoke control, fire throttling (extra resistance of hot gas), backlayering length and tenability (temperature, visibility). These need a network/transient model such as SES or a CFD study; this Phase 1 tool is for early sizing and checks, to be reviewed by a tunnel ventilation engineer.
